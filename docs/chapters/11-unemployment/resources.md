## Key Academic Papers

### Search and Matching Theory

- **Mortensen, Dale T., and Christopher A. Pissarides.** "Job Creation and Job Destruction in the Theory of Unemployment." *Review of Economic Studies* 61(3): 397--415, 1994. [DOI: 10.2307/2297896](https://doi.org/10.2307/2297896)
    - The foundational model of equilibrium unemployment with endogenous job creation and destruction. The Mortensen-Pissarides framework became the workhorse model in macrolabour economics. (Nobel Prize in Economics, 2010.)

- **Rogerson, Richard, Robert Shimer, and Randall Wright.** "Search-Theoretic Models of the Labor Market: A Survey." *Journal of Economic Literature* 43(4): 959--988, 2005. [DOI: 10.1257/002205105775362014](https://doi.org/10.1257/002205105775362014)
    - Comprehensive survey of search and matching models, covering wage determination, efficiency, and policy applications.

- **Shimer, Robert.** "The Cyclical Behavior of Equilibrium Unemployment and Vacancies." *American Economic Review* 95(1): 25--49, 2005. [DOI: 10.1257/0002828053828572](https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828053828572)
    - Shows that the standard Mortensen-Pissarides model cannot replicate the observed volatility of unemployment and vacancies over the business cycle --- the "Shimer puzzle."

### Unemployment Insurance

- **Chetty, Raj.** "Moral Hazard vs. Liquidity as Explanations for Unemployment Insurance." *NBER Working Paper No. 11760*, 2008. [DOI: 10.3386/w11760](https://doi.org/10.3386/w11760)
    - Separates the moral hazard and liquidity effects of UI. Finds that about 60% of the increase in unemployment duration from higher UI reflects the liquidity effect (consumption smoothing) rather than moral hazard (reduced search effort).

- **Card, David, Raj Chetty, and Andrea Weber.** "Cash-on-Hand and Competing Models of Intertemporal Behavior: New Evidence from the Labor Market." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 122(4): 1511--1560, 2007. [DOI: 10.1162/qjec.2007.122.4.1511](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.2007.122.4.1511)
    - Exploits a discontinuity in Austrian severance pay to estimate the liquidity effect of cash-on-hand on unemployment duration. Workers with more liquidity search longer, consistent with the liquidity interpretation.

- **Card, David, Jochen Kluve, and Andrea Weber.** "What Works? A Meta Analysis of Recent Active Labor Market Program Evaluations." *Journal of the European Economic Association* 16(3): 894--931, 2018. [DOI: 10.1093/jeea/jvx028](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvx028)
    - Meta-analysis of 207 active labour market programme evaluations. Finds that job search assistance is the most cost-effective, while subsidised public employment has limited long-term effects.

- **Baily, Martin Neil.** "Some Aspects of Optimal Unemployment Insurance." *Journal of Public Economics* 10(3): 379--402, 1978. [DOI: 10.1016/0047-2727(78)90053-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0047-2727(78)90053-1)
    - Derives the optimal UI replacement rate formula, balancing insurance value against moral hazard. The Baily-Chetty formula remains the benchmark in the optimal UI literature.

### Duration Dependence and Stigma

- **Kroft, Kory, Fabian Lange, and Matthew J. Notowidigdo.** "Duration Dependence and Labor Market Conditions: Evidence from a Field Experiment." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 128(3): 1123--1167, 2013. [DOI: 10.1093/qje/qjt015](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjt015)
    - Correspondence study showing that employers discriminate against applicants with longer unemployment spells. The effect is stronger in tight labour markets, suggesting employer screening rather than true skill depreciation.

### Phillips Curve and NAIRU

- **Phillips, A. W.** "The Relation Between Unemployment and the Rate of Change of Money Wage Rates in the United Kingdom, 1861--1957." *Economica* 25(100): 283--299, 1958. [DOI: 10.1111/j.1468-0335.1958.tb00003.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0335.1958.tb00003.x)
    - The original empirical documentation of the inverse relationship between unemployment and wage growth.

- **Friedman, Milton.** "The Role of Monetary Policy." *American Economic Review* 58(1): 1--17, 1968.
    - Introduces the natural rate hypothesis and the expectations-augmented Phillips curve. Argues that the long-run Phillips curve is vertical.

## Indian Context

### Unemployment Measurement and Trends

- **Abraham, Vinoj.** "Employment Growth in Rural India: Distress-Driven?" *Economic and Political Weekly* 44(16): 97--104, 2009.
    - Analyses rural employment patterns and distinguishes between productive employment and distress-driven self-employment in Indian data.

- **Kannan, K. P., and G. Raveendran.** "Growth Sans Employment: A Quarter Century of Jobless Growth in India's Organised Manufacturing." *Economic and Political Weekly* 44(10): 80--91, 2009.
    - Documents the divergence between GDP growth and employment growth in Indian manufacturing --- a central puzzle in India's labour market.

- **Himanshu.** "Recent Trends in Poverty and Inequality: Some Preliminary Results." *Economic and Political Weekly* 42(6): 497--508, 2007.
    - Contextualises unemployment within the broader framework of poverty and inequality in India.

### Youth Unemployment

- **Mehrotra, Santosh, and Jajati K. Parida.** "Why Is the Labour Force Participation of Women Declining in India?" *World Development* 98: 360--380, 2017. [DOI: 10.1016/j.worlddev.2017.05.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.worlddev.2017.05.003)
    - Analyses declining female LFPR and its connection to unemployment measurement and labour market slack.

- **Subramanian, S., and D. Jayaraj.** "The Distribution of Household Wealth in India." UNU-WIDER Research Paper, 2006.
    - Provides context on wealth inequality and its implications for job search behaviour and liquidity constraints among Indian workers.

### NREGA

- **Imbert, Clement, and John Papp.** "Labor Market Effects of Social Programs: Evidence from India's Employment Guarantee." *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* 7(2): 233--263, 2015. [DOI: 10.1257/app.20130401](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130401)
    - Estimates that NREGA increased private-sector wages by 4.7% and reduced private employment, confirming the programme's role as an outside option.

- **Zimmermann, Laura.** "Why Guarantee Employment? Evidence from a Large Indian Public-Works Program." Working Paper, 2020.
    - Examines NREGA's effectiveness as insurance against income shocks and its impact on consumption smoothing.

- **Dreze, Jean, and Reetika Khera.** "The Battle for Employment Guarantee." *Frontline* 26(1), 2009.
    - Accessible overview of NREGA's design, implementation challenges, and political economy.

### CMIE Data

- **Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy (CMIE).** Consumer Pyramids Household Survey (CPHS). Various waves.
    - High-frequency panel data providing alternative unemployment estimates for India. CMIE often reports higher unemployment rates than PLFS, partly due to methodological differences in measuring employment status.

## Data Sources

| Source | Coverage | Key Variables |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| **PLFS** (Periodic Labour Force Survey) | Annual + quarterly, all-India | Unemployment (UPSS, CWS, CDS), age, education, state |
| **CMIE-CPHS** (Consumer Pyramids) | Three waves/year, panel | Employment status, income, consumption, high-frequency |
| **BLS/CPS** (US Bureau of Labor Statistics) | Monthly | Unemployment rate, duration, flows (JOLTS) |
| **JOLTS** (Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey) | Monthly, US | Hires, separations, quits, vacancies --- for flow analysis |
| **OECD Labour Force Statistics** | Annual/quarterly | Cross-country unemployment rates, active labour market policies |
| **Eurostat** | Monthly/quarterly, EU | Unemployment by duration, youth unemployment |

## Stata Exercises

!!! stata "Calculating Unemployment Rates from PLFS"
    ```stata
    * Load PLFS person-level data
    use plfs_person_2024, clear

    * Define unemployment under CWS
    * (Adjust variable names to match your PLFS extract)
    gen unemployed_cws = (cws_status >= 81 & cws_status <= 82)
    gen in_lf_cws = (cws_status >= 11 & cws_status <= 82)

    * Overall CWS unemployment rate
    sum unemployed_cws if in_lf_cws == 1 [aw = weight]

    * Unemployment rate by age group
    gen age_group = .
    replace age_group = 1 if age >= 15 & age <= 29
    replace age_group = 2 if age >= 30 & age <= 44
    replace age_group = 3 if age >= 45 & age <= 59
    label define agegrp 1 "Youth (15-29)" 2 "Prime (30-44)" 3 "Older (45-59)"
    label values age_group agegrp

    table age_group [aw = weight], ///
        stat(mean unemployed_cws) nformat(%9.3f)

    * Unemployment by education level
    table education_level [aw = weight] if in_lf_cws == 1, ///
        stat(mean unemployed_cws) nformat(%9.3f)
    ```

!!! stata "Steady-State Unemployment Simulation"
    ```stata
    * Simulate labour market flows to verify steady-state formula
    clear
    set obs 100  // 100 time periods

    gen t = _n
    scalar s = 0.02  // separation rate
    scalar f = 0.20  // finding rate
    scalar L = 1000000  // labour force

    * Steady-state prediction
    scalar u_star = s / (s + f)
    display "Predicted steady-state rate: " u_star

    * Simulate dynamics starting from full employment
    gen U = 0 in 1
    gen E = L in 1

    forvalues i = 2/100 {
        quietly replace U = U[`=`i'-1'] + s * E[`=`i'-1'] - f * U[`=`i'-1'] in `i'
        quietly replace E = L - U in `i'
    }

    gen u_rate = U / L
    line u_rate t, yline(`=u_star') ///
        title("Convergence to Steady-State Unemployment Rate") ///
        ytitle("Unemployment Rate") xtitle("Period") ///
        note("Dashed line = predicted steady state")
    ```

!!! stata "Unemployment Duration Analysis"
    ```stata

    stcox age education female ui_recipient region_urban

    * Negative coefficients = slower exit (longer spells)

    * Test for duration dependence
    streg age education female ui_recipient, dist(weibull)
    * Shape parameter p < 1 indicates negative duration dependence
    * Shape parameter p > 1 indicates positive duration dependence
    ```
