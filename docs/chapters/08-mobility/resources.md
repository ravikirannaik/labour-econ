## Key Academic Papers

### Migration Theory and Self-Selection

- **Roy, A.D. (1951).** "Some Thoughts on the Distribution of Earnings." *Oxford Economic Papers*, 3(2), 135--146. [doi:10.1093/oxfordjournals.oep.a041827](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.oep.a041827) --- The original model of occupational self-selection. Workers sort into occupations (or, by extension, locations) based on comparative advantage. Foundation of the Borjas migration selection framework.

- **Borjas, George J. (1987).** "Self-Selection and the Earnings of Immigrants." *American Economic Review*, 77(4), 531--553. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1814529) --- Applies the Roy model to international migration. Shows that the skill composition of immigrants depends on the relative inequality of origin and destination countries. Positively selected when the destination is more unequal; negatively selected when the destination is more equal.

- **Sjaastad, Larry A. (1962).** "The Costs and Returns of Human Migration." *Journal of Political Economy*, 70(5, Part 2), 80--93. [doi:10.1086/258726](https://doi.org/10.1086/258726) --- The classic paper that formalised migration as a human capital investment. Introduced the cost-benefit framework used throughout the chapter.

### Empirical Studies of Immigration

- **Card, David (1990).** "The Impact of the Mariel Boatlift on the Miami Labor Market." *ILR Review*, 43(2), 245--257. [doi:10.1177/001979399004300205](https://doi.org/10.1177/001979399004300205) --- Found no significant wage or employment effects on native workers in Miami after the sudden arrival of 125,000 Cuban immigrants. A landmark natural experiment in the immigration literature.

- **Borjas, George J. (2003).** "The Labor Demand Curve Is Downward Sloping: Reexamining the Impact of Immigration on the Labor Market." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 118(4), 1335--1374. [doi:10.1162/003355303322552810](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355303322552810) --- Argues that when the analysis is conducted at the national level (rather than city-level), immigration significantly depresses wages of competing native workers. Estimated a 3--4% wage decline for a 10% increase in labour supply.

- **Card, David (2001).** "Immigrant Inflows, Native Outflows, and the Local Labor Market Impacts of Higher Immigration." *Journal of Labor Economics*, 19(1), 22--64. [doi:10.1086/209979](https://doi.org/10.1086/209979) --- Examines whether native workers leave cities that receive large immigrant inflows (the "displacement" hypothesis). Finds limited evidence of native outmigration, suggesting that local labour markets absorb immigrants through other mechanisms.

- **Clemens, Michael A. (2011).** "Economics and Emigration: Trillion-Dollar Bills on the Sidewalk?" *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 25(3), 83--106. [doi:10.1257/jep.25.3.83](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.25.3.83) --- Argues that the welfare gains from removing international migration barriers would be enormous --- potentially doubling world GDP. A provocative and accessible overview of the efficiency case for open borders.

### Job Turnover and Match Quality

- **Topel, Robert H., and Michael P. Ward (1992).** "Job Mobility and the Careers of Young Men." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 107(2), 439--479. [doi:10.2307/2118478](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118478) --- Documents that young US workers hold an average of 7 jobs in their first 10 years and that about one-third of wage growth comes from job-to-job transitions. Evidence that job shopping improves match quality.

- **Jovanovic, Boyan (1979).** "Job Matching and the Theory of Turnover." *Journal of Political Economy*, 87(5, Part 1), 972--990. [doi:10.1086/260808](https://doi.org/10.1086/260808) --- The theoretical foundation of the match-quality model. Workers and firms learn about match quality over time; bad matches dissolve (turnover) while good matches persist.

## Indian Context

### Academic Papers

- **Munshi, Kaivan, and Mark Rosenzweig (2016).** "Networks and Misallocation: Insurance, Migration, and the Rural-Urban Wage Gap." *American Economic Review*, 106(1), 46--98. [doi:10.1257/aer.20131365](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20131365) --- Shows that caste-based insurance networks in rural India discourage migration to cities because leaving the village means losing access to informal insurance. This network effect keeps workers in low-productivity rural areas and contributes to the rural-urban wage gap.

- **Kone, Zovanga L., Maggie Y. Liu, Aaditya Mattoo, Caglar Ozden, and Siddharth Sharma (2018).** "Internal Borders and Migration in India." *Journal of Economic Geography*, 18(4), 729--759. [doi:10.1093/jeg/lbx045](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeg/lbx045) --- Quantifies barriers to interstate migration in India: language, caste, religion, and state-specific entitlements all reduce mobility. Estimates that India's internal migration rate is significantly below the level predicted by its regional wage differences.

- **Imbert, Clement, and John Papp (2015).** "Labor Market Effects of Social Programs: Evidence from India's Employment Guarantee." *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*, 7(2), 233--263. [doi:10.1257/app.20130401](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130401) --- Shows that NREGA reduced seasonal out-migration from rural areas by providing employment at home during the off-season. Demonstrates that migration decisions respond to changes in the opportunity cost of staying.

- **Deshingkar, Priya, and Shaheen Akter (2009).** "Migration and Human Development in India." *UNDP Human Development Research Paper* 2009/13. [UNDP](https://hdr.undp.org/content/migration-and-human-development-india) --- Comprehensive overview of internal migration patterns in India, including seasonal/circular migration, gendered dimensions, and the role of caste and class.

- **Srivastava, Ravi (2020).** "Understanding Circular Migration in India." *Indian Council of Social Science Research*. --- Analyses the scale, patterns, and policy implications of circular migration, emphasising the vulnerability of migrant workers and the failure of urban governance to accommodate them.

### Data Sources

- **Census of India (2011) --- Migration Tables** --- D-series tables provide data on migration by place of last residence, duration of residence, and reason for migration (work, education, marriage, family). Available at [censusindia.gov.in](https://censusindia.gov.in/). The 2021 Census has not yet been conducted.

- **Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** --- The PLFS migration module (annual survey) captures migration status, duration, and reason. Available at [mospi.gov.in](https://mospi.gov.in/). See also the [PLFS Data Guide](../../indian-context/plfs-overview.md) and the [Migration page](../../indian-context/migration.md) on this site.

- **Economic Survey of India** --- Annual publication by the Ministry of Finance. Chapter on migration in the 2016-17 Economic Survey estimated interstate migration at approximately 9 million per year using railway passenger data. Available at [indiabudget.gov.in](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/).

- **National Sample Survey (NSS) --- Migration Rounds** --- The 64th Round (2007-08) is the most recent dedicated migration survey. Provides detailed data on reasons for migration, remittances, and conditions at destination.

## Stata Exercises

!!! stata "Analysing Migration with PLFS Data"
    ```stata
    * Load PLFS person-level data
    use plfs_person_2024.dta, clear

    * Identify migrants (those whose last usual residence differs from current)
    gen migrant = (migration_status == 1)

    * Migration rates by age group
    gen agegroup = floor(age/5) * 5
    table agegroup, c(mean migrant) format(%9.3f)

    * Migration rates by education level
    table education_level, c(mean migrant) format(%9.3f)

    * Wage comparison: migrants vs non-migrants (regular salaried workers)
    keep if status_code == 31 & wage > 0
    ttest ln(wage), by(migrant)

    * Regression: wage premium for migrants controlling for education and age
    gen lnwage = ln(wage)
    reg lnwage migrant i.education_level age agesq i.sector, robust
    ```

    The migration module in PLFS is available in the annual report data files. Migration status, reason for migration, and duration of residence are key variables.

!!! stata "Job Tenure and Turnover"
    ```stata
    * Analyse job tenure distribution
    use plfs_person_2024.dta, clear
    keep if status_code == 31  // Regular salaried workers

    * Summarise tenure
    summarize tenure, detail

    * Tenure-wage relationship
    gen lnwage = ln(wage)
    reg lnwage tenure tenure_sq i.education_level age agesq, robust

    * Quit rates by tenure category
    gen tenure_cat = cond(tenure < 1, 1, cond(tenure < 3, 2, ///
                   cond(tenure < 5, 3, cond(tenure < 10, 4, 5))))
    label define tcat 1 "<1 year" 2 "1-3 years" 3 "3-5 years" ///
                       4 "5-10 years" 5 "10+ years"
    label values tenure_cat tcat
    table tenure_cat, c(mean changed_job) format(%9.3f)
    ```

## Additional Reading

- **Dustmann, Christian, and Joseph-Simon Gorlach (2016).** "The Economics of Temporary Migrations." *Journal of Economic Literature*, 54(1), 98--136. [doi:10.1257/jel.54.1.98](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.54.1.98) --- Develops theory and evidence on temporary and return migration, directly relevant to India's circular migration patterns.

- **Barro, Robert J., and Xavier Sala-i-Martin (1992).** "Convergence." *Journal of Political Economy*, 100(2), 223--251. [doi:10.1086/261816](https://doi.org/10.1086/261816) --- Classic paper documenting the rate of regional income convergence across US states, with migration as a key mechanism.

- **Borjas, George J. (1995).** "The Economic Benefits from Immigration." *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 9(2), 3--22. [doi:10.1257/jep.9.2.3](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.9.2.3) --- Accessible derivation of the immigration surplus framework, showing that the net gains to natives are small but the redistribution from workers to capital owners is large.

- **Economic Survey of India 2016-17, Chapter 12: "India on the Move and Churning."** [indiabudget.gov.in](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2017-2018/es2016-17/echap12.pdf) --- Uses railway passenger data and PLFS to estimate interstate migration flows and argues that migration in India has been increasing. One of the most cited policy documents on Indian internal migration.
