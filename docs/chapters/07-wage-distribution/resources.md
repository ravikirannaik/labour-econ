## Key Academic Papers

These papers form the intellectual backbone of the wage inequality literature. The first three are essential; the remainder provide depth on specific mechanisms.

### Foundational

- **Goldin, Claudia, and Lawrence F. Katz (2008).** *The Race between Education and Technology.* Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press. --- The definitive historical analysis of the interplay between education and technology in shaping the US wage distribution over the 20th century. [Harvard University Press](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674035300)

- **Autor, David H., Lawrence F. Katz, and Melissa S. Kearney (2008).** "Trends in U.S. Wage Inequality: Revising the Revisionists." *Review of Economics and Statistics*, 90(2), 300--323. [doi:10.1162/rest.90.2.300](https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.90.2.300) --- Documents job polarisation --- the hollowing-out of middle-skill employment --- and distinguishes between upper-tail and lower-tail inequality trends.

- **Piketty, Thomas, and Emmanuel Saez (2003).** "Income Inequality in the United States, 1913--1998." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 118(1), 1--41. [doi:10.1162/00335530360535135](https://doi.org/10.1162/00335530360535135) --- Uses tax data to document the dramatic rise in top income shares. Regularly updated at the [World Inequality Database](https://wid.world/).

### SBTC and Technological Change

- **Tinbergen, Jan (1975).** *Income Distribution: Analysis and Policies.* Amsterdam: North-Holland. --- Originated the "race between education and technology" framework.

- **Katz, Lawrence F., and Kevin M. Murphy (1992).** "Changes in Relative Wages, 1963--1987: Supply and Demand Factors." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 107(1), 35--78. [doi:10.2307/2118323](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118323) --- Formalises the supply-demand framework for the skill premium that underlies much of the subsequent literature.

- **Autor, David H., Frank Levy, and Richard J. Murnane (2003).** "The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change: An Empirical Exploration." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 118(4), 1279--1333. [doi:10.1162/003355303322552801](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355303322552801) --- Develops the task-based framework distinguishing routine from non-routine tasks, which underpins the polarisation hypothesis.

### Superstar Markets

- **Rosen, Sherwin (1981).** "The Economics of Superstars." *American Economic Review*, 71(5), 845--858. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1803469) --- The original theoretical model explaining why small talent differences produce enormous earnings differences in markets with imperfect substitution and scale technology.

### Institutions

- **Card, David, and Alan B. Krueger (1994).** "Minimum Wages and Employment: A Case Study of the Fast-Food Industry in New Jersey and Pennsylvania." *American Economic Review*, 84(4), 772--793. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2118030) --- Landmark study finding no significant employment effects from a minimum wage increase.

- **DiNardo, John, Nicole M. Fortin, and Thomas Lemieux (1996).** "Labor Market Institutions and the Distribution of Wages, 1973--1992: A Semiparametric Approach." *Econometrica*, 64(5), 1001--1044. [doi:10.2307/2171954](https://doi.org/10.2307/2171954) --- Quantifies the contributions of the minimum wage, unions, and supply-demand shifts to changes in the US wage distribution.

## Indian Context

### Academic Papers

- **Kijima, Yoko (2006).** "Why Did Wage Inequality Increase? Evidence from Urban India 1983--1999." *Journal of Development Economics*, 81(1), 97--117. [doi:10.1016/j.jdeveco.2005.04.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2005.04.008) --- Documents rising returns to education in India during the liberalisation period, with the largest increases for tertiary education. Finds that between-group inequality (education) drove the increase.

- **Azam, Mehtabul (2012).** "Changes in Wage Structure in Urban India, 1983--2004: A Quantile Regression Decomposition." *World Development*, 40(6), 1135--1150. [doi:10.1016/j.worlddev.2012.01.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.worlddev.2012.01.004) --- Uses quantile regression to show that the college premium in India rose across the entire distribution, with the largest increases at the top.

- **Madheswaran, S., and Paul Attewell (2007).** "Caste Discrimination in the Indian Urban Labour Market: Evidence from the National Sample Survey." *Economic and Political Weekly*, 42(41), 4146--4153. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/40276551) --- Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition reveals a substantial "unexplained" caste wage gap consistent with discrimination.

- **Dutta, Puja Vasudeva (2006).** "Returns to Education: New Evidence for India, 1983--1999." *Education Economics*, 14(4), 431--451. [doi:10.1080/09645290600854128](https://doi.org/10.1080/09645290600854128) --- Finds that Mincerian returns to education rose significantly during the 1990s, particularly for higher education and in urban areas.

### Data Sources

- **Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** --- Annual and quarterly survey by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). Provides detailed data on wages, employment status, education, industry, and occupation. Available at [mospi.gov.in](https://mospi.gov.in/). See also the [PLFS Data Guide](../../indian-context/plfs-overview.md) on this site.

- **World Inequality Database (WID)** --- India income distribution data including top income shares, Gini coefficients, and historical trends. [wid.world/country/india](https://wid.world/country/india/)

- **India Human Development Survey (IHDS)** --- Panel data (2005, 2011-12) covering income, wages, education, and caste for over 40,000 households. Available at [ihds.umd.edu](https://ihds.umd.edu/).

## Stata Exercises

!!! stata "Measuring Inequality with PLFS Data"
    ```stata
    * Load PLFS wage data
    use plfs_wages_2024.dta, clear

    * Keep regular salaried workers with positive wages
    keep if status_code == 31 & wage > 0

    * Log wages
    gen lnwage = ln(wage)

    * Gini coefficient
    ineqdeco wage

    * Percentile ratios
    quietly summarize wage, detail
    display "90/10 = " r(p90) / r(p10)
    display "90/50 = " r(p90) / r(p50)
    display "50/10 = " r(p50) / r(p10)

    * Variance decomposition by education
    ineqdeco lnwage, bygroup(education_level)

    * Kernel density of log wages by education
    twoway (kdensity lnwage if education_level <= 2, lcolor(red)) ///
           (kdensity lnwage if education_level >= 3, lcolor(blue)), ///
           legend(label(1 "Below graduation") label(2 "Graduate+")) ///
           xtitle("Log hourly wage") ytitle("Density")
    ```

    The `ineqdeco` command (from SSC) computes the Gini, Theil index, and variance decomposition in a single step. Install with `ssc install ineqdeco`.

## Additional Reading

- **Autor, David H. (2014).** "Skills, Education, and the Rise of Earnings Inequality among the 'Other 99 Percent.'" *Science*, 344(6186), 843--851. [doi:10.1126/science.1251868](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1251868) --- Accessible overview distinguishing inequality at the top (superstars) from inequality among the rest (SBTC and polarisation).

- **Acemoglu, Daron, and David Autor (2011).** "Skills, Tasks, and Technologies: Implications for Employment and Earnings." In *Handbook of Labor Economics*, Vol. 4B, 1043--1171. [doi:10.1016/S0169-7218(11)02410-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-7218(11)02410-5) --- The canonical theoretical and empirical survey of the task-based framework for understanding wage inequality.

- **Chancel, Lucas, and Thomas Piketty (2019).** "Indian Income Inequality, 1922--2015: From British Raj to Billionaire Raj?" *Review of Income and Wealth*, 65(S1), S33--S62. [doi:10.1111/roiw.12439](https://doi.org/10.1111/roiw.12439) --- Documents the rise in top income shares in India since liberalisation, with comparisons to the colonial period.
