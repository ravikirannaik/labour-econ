## Key Academic Papers

### Piece Rates and Performance Pay

- **Lazear, Edward P.** "Performance Pay and Productivity." *American Economic Review* 90(5): 1346--1361, 2000. [DOI: 10.1257/aer.90.5.1346](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.90.5.1346)
    - Landmark study of Safelite Glass Corporation's switch from hourly wages to piece rates. Productivity increased by 44%, split between incentive and sorting effects.

- **Shearer, Bruce.** "Piece Rates, Fixed Wages and Incentives: Evidence from a Field Experiment." *Review of Economic Studies* 71(2): 513--534, 2004. [DOI: 10.1111/0034-6527.00294](https://doi.org/10.1111/0034-6527.00294)
    - Field experiment with tree planters in British Columbia. Workers randomly assigned to piece rates planted 20% more trees per day than those on fixed wages.

- **Prendergast, Canice.** "The Provision of Incentives in Firms." *Journal of Economic Literature* 37(1): 7--63, 1999. [DOI: 10.1257/jel.37.1.7](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.37.1.7)
    - Comprehensive survey of incentive pay theory and evidence, covering moral hazard, multi-tasking, career concerns, and subjective evaluation.

### Efficiency Wages

- **Shapiro, Carl, and Joseph E. Stiglitz.** "Equilibrium Unemployment as a Worker Discipline Device." *American Economic Review* 74(3): 433--444, 1984.
    - The canonical shirking model of efficiency wages. Shows that above-market wages and unemployment are equilibrium features when monitoring is imperfect.

- **Akerlof, George A.** "Labor Contracts as Partial Gift Exchange." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 97(4): 543--569, 1982. [DOI: 10.2307/1885099](https://doi.org/10.2307/1885099)
    - The gift-exchange model: firms pay above-market wages as a "gift," and workers reciprocate with higher effort. Draws on sociological norms of fairness.

- **Raff, Daniel M. G., and Lawrence H. Summers.** "Did Henry Ford Pay Efficiency Wages?" *Journal of Labor Economics* 5(4): S57--S86, 1987. [DOI: 10.1086/298165](https://doi.org/10.1086/298165)
    - Historical case study of Ford's famous $5/day wage in 1914. Finds evidence consistent with efficiency wage theory: turnover dropped dramatically and productivity rose.

### Tournaments

- **Lazear, Edward P., and Sherwin Rosen.** "Rank-Order Tournaments as Optimum Labor Contracts." *Journal of Political Economy* 89(5): 841--864, 1981. [DOI: 10.1086/261010](https://doi.org/10.1086/261010)
    - Foundational paper on tournament theory. Shows that rank-order tournaments can elicit efficient effort and that prize spreads increase with the number of competitors and the variance of luck.

- **Ehrenberg, Ronald G., and Michael L. Bognanno.** "Do Tournaments Have Incentive Effects?" *Journal of Political Economy* 98(6): 1307--1324, 1990. [DOI: 10.1086/261736](https://doi.org/10.1086/261736)
    - Tests tournament theory using data from professional golf tournaments. Finds that players score better in tournaments with larger prizes.

### Executive Compensation

- **Gabaix, Xavier, and Augustin Landier.** "Why Has CEO Pay Increased So Much?" *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 123(1): 49--100, 2008. [DOI: 10.1162/qjec.2008.123.1.49](https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.2008.123.1.49)
    - Argues that the sixfold increase in CEO pay between 1980 and 2003 is explained by the sixfold increase in firm market capitalisation. Small talent differences generate large pay differences when firms are large.

- **Bebchuk, Lucian A., and Jesse M. Fried.** *Pay Without Performance: The Unfulfilled Promise of Executive Compensation*. Harvard University Press, 2004.
    - The managerial power critique: argues that CEO pay reflects rent extraction rather than optimal contracting. Weak boards allow CEOs to set their own pay.

### Team Production

- **Alchian, Armen A., and Harold Demsetz.** "Production, Information Costs, and Economic Organization." *American Economic Review* 62(5): 777--795, 1972.
    - Argues that the firm exists to solve the free-rider problem in team production through monitoring by a residual claimant.

- **Holmstrom, Bengt.** "Moral Hazard in Teams." *Bell Journal of Economics* 13(2): 324--340, 1982. [DOI: 10.2307/3003457](https://doi.org/10.2307/3003457)
    - Shows that balanced budget-sharing in teams cannot achieve first-best effort. Introduces budget-breaking as a solution.

## Indian Context

### Informal Sector Wage Structures

- **Harriss-White, Barbara.** *India Working: Essays on Society and Economy*. Cambridge University Press, 2003.
    - Detailed analysis of informal sector employment relationships, including piece-rate systems and the role of intermediaries in setting wages.

- **Srivastava, Ravi.** "Bonded Labor in India: Its Incidence and Pattern." ILO Working Paper, 2005.
    - Documents tied and bonded labour arrangements where wage incentives are distorted by debt and coercion.

### NREGA

- **Imbert, Clement, and John Papp.** "Labor Market Effects of Social Programs: Evidence from India's Employment Guarantee." *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics* 7(2): 233--263, 2015. [DOI: 10.1257/app.20130401](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130401)
    - Finds that NREGA raised private-sector wages by 4.7% and reduced private-sector employment, consistent with the programme acting as an outside option that raises the reservation wage.

- **Muralidharan, Karthik, Paul Niehaus, and Sandip Sukhtankar.** "Building State Capacity: Evidence from Biometric Smartcards in India." *American Economic Review* 106(10): 2895--2929, 2016. [DOI: 10.1257/aer.20141346](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20141346)
    - Studies the effect of biometric payment systems on NREGA wage delivery, reducing leakage and improving incentive alignment in public programmes.

## Data Sources

| Source | Coverage | Key Variables |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| **PLFS** (Periodic Labour Force Survey) | Annual, all-India | Wage type (time-rated, piece-rated), earnings, occupation, industry |
| **ASI** (Annual Survey of Industries) | Annual, factory sector | Wages, bonuses, emoluments, output per worker |
| **NSS Employment Rounds** | Quinquennial | Detailed wage structure by payment type |
| **ExecuComp** (US) | Annual, S&P 1500 firms | CEO compensation: salary, bonus, stock options, total pay |
| **Compustat** (US) | Annual | Firm-level data for linking pay to performance |

## Stata Exercises

!!! stata "Analysing Wage Structures in PLFS Data"
    ```stata
    * Load PLFS person-level data
    use plfs_person_2024, clear

    * Identify payment types
    tab wage_type
    * Common categories: monthly salary, daily wage, piece rate

    * Compare earnings across payment types
    tabstat monthly_earnings, by(wage_type) stat(mean median sd n)

    * Piece-rate workers vs. time-rate workers: earnings equation
    gen piece_rate = (wage_type == 3)  // adjust code as per PLFS codebook
    reg ln_earnings educ exper exper2 piece_rate i.industry i.state

    * Interaction: does piece rate premium vary by industry?
    reg ln_earnings educ exper exper2 piece_rate##i.industry i.state
    ```

!!! stata "Testing Tournament Theory with Sports Data"
    ```stata
    * Example using golf tournament data
    * (Illustrative — adapt to available dataset)
    use golf_tournaments, clear

    * Does prize money predict performance?
    reg final_score total_purse player_ranking course_difficulty
    * Expect: higher purse → lower score (better performance)

    * Test for round-specific effects (effort increases in later rounds)
    reg round_score i.round##c.total_purse player_ranking
    ```
