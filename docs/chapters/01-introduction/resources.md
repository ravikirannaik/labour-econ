## Key Papers

!!! empirical "Foundational Papers on Labour Economics as a Field"

    - **Hamermesh, Daniel S.** "Viewpoint: Fun with matched firm-employee data." *Canadian Journal of Economics*, 37(2), 2004, pp. 257-274. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0008-4085.2004.00006.x)
        - *A reflection on what labour economists do, how the field has evolved, and what kinds of data and questions define it. Useful for understanding why labour economics is a distinct subfield.*

    - **Autor, David H.** "Why Are There Still So Many Jobs? The History and Future of Workplace Automation." *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 29(3), 2015, pp. 3-30. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.29.3.3)
        - *An accessible overview of how labour markets have adapted to technological change throughout history. Directly relevant to the "why study labour economics?" motivation -- labour markets are always in flux.*

    - **Freeman, Richard B.** "Labor Economics." In *The New Palgrave Dictionary of Economics*, 2nd edition, 2008.
        - *A concise survey of the field, its major questions, and its evolution from institutional description to empirical causal analysis.*

!!! empirical "Labour Market Trends and Inequality"

    - **Katz, Lawrence F. and Kevin M. Murphy.** "Changes in Relative Wages, 1963-1987: Supply and Demand Factors." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 107(1), 1992, pp. 35-78. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118323)
        - *The classic supply-demand framework for understanding the rise in the college wage premium. Sets up the analytical approach used throughout the Borjas textbook.*

    - **Goldin, Claudia and Lawrence F. Katz.** *The Race Between Education and Technology.* Harvard University Press, 2008.
        - *Book-length treatment of how the interplay between skill supply (education) and skill demand (technology) has shaped wage inequality in the US over the twentieth century.*

## Indian Context

!!! indian "India's Labour Market -- Data and Reports"

    - **PLFS Annual Report 2023-24.** Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, Government of India.
        - [Download from MoSPI website](https://www.mospi.gov.in/)
        - *The primary source of labour market statistics in India. Reports LFPR, unemployment, employment-population ratio, and sectoral composition by gender, age, education, and urban/rural status.*

    - **ILO.** *India Employment Report 2024: Youth employment, education and skills.* International Labour Organization, Geneva, 2024.
        - [ILO India Employment Report](https://www.ilo.org/india)
        - *Comprehensive overview of India's labour market challenges, with a focus on youth employment, the informal economy, and skill mismatches.*

    - **Mehrotra, Santosh and Jajati K. Parida.** "India's Employment Crisis: Rising Education Levels and Falling Non-Farm Job Creation." *Centre for Sustainable Employment Working Paper No. 2019-04*, Azim Premji University, 2019.
        - *Analyses the structural transformation challenge: education levels rising, but non-agricultural job creation lagging behind.*

    - **Kannan, K.P. and G. Raveendran.** "Growth sans Employment: A Quarter Century of Jobless Growth in India's Organised Manufacturing." *Economic and Political Weekly*, 44(10), 2009, pp. 80-91.
        - *Documents the decoupling of output growth and employment growth in Indian manufacturing -- a key puzzle for labour economists studying India.*

## Glossary Terms Used in This Chapter

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Labour force** | All working-age persons who are either employed or unemployed (actively seeking work) |
| **Labour force participation rate (LFPR)** | The fraction of the working-age population that is in the labour force |
| **Unemployment rate** | The fraction of the labour force that is unemployed |
| **Employed** | A person who worked for pay or profit during the reference period (even for one hour) |
| **Unemployed** | A person who did not work during the reference period but was actively searching for work and available to work |
| **Not in the labour force (NILF)** | Persons of working age who are neither employed nor unemployed (e.g., students, retirees, homemakers, discouraged workers) |
| **Positive economics** | Analysis of what *is* -- testable claims about cause and effect in the economy |
| **Normative economics** | Analysis of what *ought to be* -- value judgements about the desirability of outcomes or policies |
| **Discouraged worker** | A person who wants work but has stopped searching because they believe no jobs are available |
| **Underemployment** | A situation where a worker has a job but works fewer hours than desired, or in a job below their skill level |
