# Chapters

This course covers 11 chapters from George J. Borjas's *Labor Economics* (8th edition, McGraw-Hill 2016). Each chapter includes a content summary, practice problems, and additional resources.

Each chapter page has three sections:

| Section | What You'll Find |
|---------|-----------------|
| :material-book-open-variant: **Content** | Key concepts, models, worked examples, and Indian context |
| :material-help-circle: **Practice** | MCQs, short-answer questions, and numerical problems with solutions |
| :material-link-variant: **Resources** | Readings, empirical papers, videos, and Stata exercises |

### Chapter 2: Labor Supply
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">5 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 2</span>

How do individuals decide whether and how much to work? This chapter develops the work-leisure model, analyses income and substitution effects, and examines labour force participation trends. Extensions cover household production models and the labour supply effects of welfare programs and the EITC.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](02-labor-supply/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](02-labor-supply/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](02-labor-supply/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 3: Labor Demand
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">5 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 3</span>

How do firms decide how many workers to hire? This chapter covers profit maximisation in both the short run and the long run, derives the marginal productivity condition, and introduces Marshall's rules of derived demand. The analysis extends to the demand for labour when firms can substitute between inputs.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](03-labor-demand/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](03-labor-demand/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](03-labor-demand/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 4: Labor Market Equilibrium
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">3 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 4</span>

What happens when labour supply meets labour demand? This chapter brings together the supply and demand frameworks to analyse competitive equilibrium, the incidence of payroll taxes, the immigration surplus, and dynamic adjustment through the cobweb model.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](04-equilibrium/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](04-equilibrium/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](04-equilibrium/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 5: Compensating Wage Differentials
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">4 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 5</span>

Why do wages differ across jobs even for similar workers? This chapter introduces hedonic wage theory, explains how compensating differentials arise from differences in job risk and amenities, and discusses the estimation of the value of a statistical life (VSL).

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](05-compensating-differentials/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](05-compensating-differentials/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](05-compensating-differentials/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 6: Education
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">4 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 6</span>

Is education an investment or a signal? This chapter develops the human capital model, derives the Mincer earnings equation, and contrasts the investment view with the signalling model. Key empirical issues include estimating returns to schooling and addressing ability bias.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](06-education/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](06-education/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](06-education/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 7: The Wage Distribution
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">4 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 7</span>

Why has wage inequality increased so dramatically? This chapter examines trends in the wage distribution, the role of skill-biased technological change (SBTC), job polarisation, and superstar markets. It also analyses the effects of the minimum wage on employment and the wage distribution.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](07-wage-distribution/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](07-wage-distribution/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](07-wage-distribution/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 8: Labor Mobility
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">3 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 8</span>

Why do workers move across jobs, firms, and regions? This chapter treats migration as a human capital investment, analyses the determinants of geographic mobility and job turnover, and discusses immigration policy and its labour market consequences.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](08-mobility/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](08-mobility/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](08-mobility/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 9: Labor Market Discrimination
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">4 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 9</span>

How do we measure and explain wage gaps across demographic groups? This chapter presents Becker's taste-based discrimination model, statistical discrimination, and the use of audit and correspondence studies. Applications cover gender, race, and caste-based wage differentials.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](09-discrimination/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](09-discrimination/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](09-discrimination/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 10: Incentive Pay
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">3 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 10</span>

How do firms design compensation to motivate workers? This chapter analyses piece rates versus time rates, tournament theory, efficiency wages, and the principal-agent problem. It examines why firms choose different pay structures and how incentive contracts affect worker effort and selection.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](10-incentive-pay/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](10-incentive-pay/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](10-incentive-pay/resources.md)
</div>

<div class="chapter-card" markdown>
### Chapter 11: Unemployment
<span class="chapter-tag hours-tag">3 Hours</span> <span class="chapter-tag borjas-tag">Borjas Ch. 11</span>

Why does unemployment persist even in a growing economy? This chapter distinguishes between frictional, structural, and cyclical unemployment, develops the job search model, and analyses the effects of unemployment insurance. It introduces the Beveridge curve and the concept of the natural rate of unemployment.

[:octicons-arrow-right-24: Content](11-unemployment/index.md) | [:octicons-pencil-24: Practice](11-unemployment/practice.md) | [:octicons-link-external-16: Resources](11-unemployment/resources.md)
