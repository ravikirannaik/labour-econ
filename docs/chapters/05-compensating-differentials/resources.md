# Resources: Compensating Wage Differentials

---

## Required Reading

- **Borjas, George J.** *Labor Economics*, 8th edition. McGraw-Hill, 2020. **Chapter 5: Compensating Wage Differentials.**

---

## Key Academic Papers

### Foundational Theory

- **Rosen, Sherwin.** "Hedonic Prices and Implicit Markets: Product Differentiation in Pure Competition." *Journal of Political Economy*, 82(1), 1974, pp. 34--55. [DOI: 10.1086/260169](https://doi.org/10.1086/260169)
    - The foundational paper on hedonic price theory. Develops the formal model of how market equilibrium determines the implicit price of product (or job) characteristics. Essential reading for understanding the theoretical underpinnings of compensating differentials.

- **Smith, Adam.** *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations*, Book I, Chapter X. 1776.
    - The original statement of compensating differentials. Smith identifies five sources of wage variation that persist in equilibrium. Available freely online via [econlib.org](https://www.econlib.org/library/Smith/smWN.html).

### Value of a Statistical Life (VSL)

- **Viscusi, W. Kip.** "The Value of Risks to Life and Health." *Journal of Economic Literature*, 31(4), 1993, pp. 1912--1946. [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2728331)
    - Comprehensive survey of the VSL literature. Discusses methods, estimates, and policy applications. A standard reference.

- **Viscusi, W. Kip and Joseph E. Aldy.** "The Value of a Statistical Life: A Critical Review of Market Estimates Throughout the World." *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty*, 27(1), 2003, pp. 5--76. [DOI: 10.1023/A:1025598106257](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1025598106257)
    - International meta-analysis of VSL estimates. Documents the cross-country pattern that VSL rises with income (elasticity 0.5--1.0).

- **Ashenfelter, Orley and Michael Greenstone.** "Using Mandated Speed Limits to Measure the Value of a Statistical Life." *Journal of Political Economy*, 112(S1), 2004, pp. S226--S267. [DOI: 10.1086/379932](https://doi.org/10.1086/379932)
    - Innovative natural experiment using changes in US speed limits. Finds a VSL of approximately $1.5 million, lower than many wage-hedonic estimates.

### Empirical Studies

- **Hwang, Hae-shin, W. Robert Reed, and Carlton Hubbard.** "Compensating Wage Differentials and Unobserved Productivity." *Journal of Political Economy*, 100(4), 1992, pp. 835--858. [DOI: 10.1086/261841](https://doi.org/10.1086/261841)
    - Demonstrates that standard hedonic regressions can produce **biased** estimates of compensating differentials if worker productivity and job amenities are correlated.

- **Bonhomme, Stephane and Gregory Jolivet.** "The Pervasive Absence of Compensating Differentials." *Journal of Applied Econometrics*, 24(5), 2009, pp. 763--795. [DOI: 10.1002/jae.1084](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.1084)
    - Uses European panel data and finds that standard cross-sectional estimates overstate compensating differentials. With worker fixed effects, many differentials disappear.

## Indian Context

- **Shanmugam, K. R.** "Self Selection Bias in the Estimates of Compensating Wage Differentials for Job Risks in India." *Journal of Quantitative Economics*, 17(1), 2001, pp. 121--138.
    - One of the few studies estimating compensating differentials using Indian manufacturing data (Madras). Finds modest positive wage-risk tradeoffs but notes the importance of correcting for self-selection.

- **Shanmugam, K. R.** "The Value of Life: Estimates from Indian Labour Market." *Indian Economic Journal*, 44(4), 1997, pp. 105--114.
    - Early estimates of VSL in India using occupational fatality data from the manufacturing sector.

- **National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB).** *Accidental Deaths & Suicides in India* (annual report).
    - Official source for occupational fatality data in India. Covers deaths in mining, construction, factory work, and other sectors. Available at [ncrb.gov.in](https://ncrb.gov.in/).

- **Srivastava, Ravi S.** "Bonded Labour in India: Its Incidence and Pattern." ILO Working Paper, 2005.
    - Documents bonded labour practices in India --- an extreme case where compensating differentials theory completely breaks down due to coercion.

- **Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013.**
    - Full text available at [legislative.gov.in](https://legislative.gov.in/). Relevant to the discussion of why hazardous, caste-segregated occupations contradict compensating differentials theory.

## Further Reading

- **Ehrenberg, Ronald G. and Robert S. Smith.** *Modern Labor Economics: Theory and Public Policy*, 13th edition. Routledge, 2018. Chapter 8: Compensating Wage Differentials and Labor Markets.
    - An alternative textbook treatment with extensive discussion of non-wage job attributes.

- **Cahuc, Pierre, Stephane Carcillo, and Andre Zylberberg.** *Labor Economics*, 2nd edition. MIT Press, 2014. Chapter 4, Section 4.2.
    - Formal treatment of the hedonic model with more mathematical detail than Borjas.
