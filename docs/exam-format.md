# End-Term Exam Format

**Total Marks: 75** | **4 Sections (A–D)** | **Syllabus: Ch 2, 5, 6, 9, 12**

---

## Overview

| Section | Type | Difficulty | Marks | Cumulative | What to Expect |
|:-------:|------|:----------:|:-----:|:----------:|----------------|
| **A** | Theory | Easy → Medium | 20 | 20 | Define, explain, justify |
| **B** | Problems | Easy → Medium | 15 | 35 | Numerical and graphical problems |
| **C** | Problems | Medium → Hard | 25 | 60 | Multi-part analytical problems |
| **D** | Applied Case Study | Open-ended | 15 | **75** | Real-world scenario requiring integrated reasoning |

---

## Section A: Theory

:material-format-quote-open: **Define, Explain, Justify**

- Short-answer conceptual questions that test your understanding of core models and ideas
- You may be asked to **define** a key term, **explain** a mechanism or result, or **justify** a theoretical claim
- Answers should be precise and concise — quality of argument matters more than length
- Diagrams are encouraged where appropriate
- **Chapters covered:** Ch 2 (Labour Supply), Ch 5 (Compensating Wage Differentials), Ch 6 (Human Capital), Ch 9 (Labour Market Discrimination), Ch 12 (Unemployment)

!!! info "Difficulty: Easy → Medium"
    These questions reward students who understand the *logic* behind the models, not just the conclusions.

---

## Section B: Problems (Easy → Medium)

:material-calculator-variant: **Numerical and Graphical Problems**

- Straightforward computational problems with clear setups
- Expect to calculate equilibrium wages, employment levels, tax incidence, compensating differentials, or rates of return
- Problems typically have a single correct answer with well-defined steps
- **Show all working** — partial credit is awarded for clear reasoning even if the final answer is wrong

!!! info "Difficulty: Easy → Medium"
    If you can work through textbook end-of-chapter problems, you are well prepared for this section.

---

## Section C: Problems (Medium → Hard)

:material-chart-scatter-plot-hexbin: **Multi-Part Analytical Problems**

- More involved problems requiring deeper analysis across multiple steps
- May combine concepts from different chapters or require you to extend a model
- Expect multi-part questions where each part builds on the previous
- Diagrams, derivations, and economic interpretation are all expected

!!! info "Difficulty: Medium → Hard"
    These problems test whether you can *apply* models to new situations, not just replicate textbook examples.

---

## Section D: Applied Case Study

:material-earth: **Open-Ended, Real-World Application**

- A case-study-based question grounded in a real labour market scenario — Indian or international
- You will need to draw on theory from the course to **analyse** the situation, **evaluate** a policy, or **argue** a position
- There is no single correct answer — what matters is the quality of your economic reasoning, use of relevant concepts, and ability to connect theory to evidence
- Approximately 200–300 words expected

!!! info "Difficulty: Open-Ended"
    This section rewards students who can think like economists — applying models to messy, real-world situations where clean answers do not exist.

---

## General Instructions

1. **Answer the question that was asked.** Veering off-topic does not signal depth — it signals that you are lost.
2. **Graphs must be clear, legible, and accurately labelled.** A diagram without labels is modern art, not economics.
3. **Show all your work.** Partial credit rewards clear reasoning, even if the final answer is wrong.
4. **Quality over quantity.** A tight, well-reasoned paragraph beats a page of vague generalities.
