    Course Description¶

    This semester-long course explores the economic aspects of labour markets, with a specific focus on market dynamics, human capital, earnings inequalities, labour mobility, and labour market discrimination. Leveraging foundational knowledge from Microeconomics, the course aims to deepen understanding of key issues in contemporary labour economics.

    Course Objectives¶

    1. Understand how labour markets work
    2. Apply core labour economics models to contemporary policy issues
    3. Interpret and critically evaluate empirical evidence in labour economics
    4. Communicate labour economics insights clearly to both economists and non-economists

    Learning Outcomes¶

    Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

    Outcome Description
    a Grasp the theoretical models of labour economics and their application
    b Gain familiarity with the empirical research in the field
    c Apply the models to significant policy issues
    d Develop the ability to comprehend journal articles in labour economics

    Textbook¶

    Required Text

    Borjas, George J. Labor Economics (8th edition). McGraw-Hill, 2016.

    The course follows the textbook chapter by chapter. You are expected to read the assigned chapters before each class. Weekly readings run 20--30 pages.

    Assessment Details¶

    Non-graded quizzes administered via Socrative (Space Run format) throughout the semester. These are designed to check understanding in real time and do not count toward your final grade.

    Group-based empirical analysis of labour market topics using Stata. Assignments are due biweekly and require you to work with real-world data, produce figures, and interpret regression output.

    An individual critical review of an assigned journal article. You will evaluate the article's research question, identification strategy, data, and conclusions. The assignment includes an AI comparison component — you will compare your critique against an AI-generated review and reflect on the differences.

    Grading Scale¶

    Grade O A+ A B+ B C P
    Minimum Score — 80 70 60 50 40 35

    Labour-based grading

    This course adopts a labour-based grading approach that centres feedback and improvement. The emphasis is on the process of learning — engaging seriously with the material, revising your work in response to feedback, and demonstrating steady progress over the semester.

    Workload Expectations¶

    This is a fast-paced course

    • 20--30 pages of mandatory reading per week from the Borjas textbook
    • Self-directed study is essential; not every topic will be covered in full during lectures
    • You are accountable for all assigned material regardless of whether it is discussed in class
    • Group Stata assignments require time outside class for data work and write-up

    Course Expectations¶

    Be Engaged. Be Curious. Work Hard.

    • Cell phones must be silenced and stowed during class
    • No late arrivals — enter before the class begins or not at all
    • No proxy attendance — attendance is verified and academic integrity violations are reported
    • No makeup exams or delayed submissions — plan ahead and communicate early if you face genuine difficulties
    • Come to class having done the reading, ready to ask questions and participate in discussion
